The Emerald Green arborvitae is an excellent choice for smaller spaces and tighter corridors. These are its key features:

Keeping Your Arborvitaes Healthy

Arborvitaes are easy to maintain, but they still need some attention to keep them in good shape. Nawada shares some tips to keep these evergreens thriving. Pruning Arborvitaes

While arborvitaes don’t require extensive pruning, some occasional trimming can help keep them healthy. Follow these tips:

Remove any dead, damaged, or diseased branches as soon as you notice them.

[All About Arborvitaes](https://all-about-arborvitaes.northlist.xyz/all-about-arborvitaes/20260822.html)

If you need to prune for shape or size control, do so in early spring before new growth emerges.

When pruning, cut at a “V” where branches intersect to encourage natural-looking regrowth.

For a formal look, shear the sides of the arborvitae in early spring.

For a more natural appearance, allow the plant to grow with minimal intervention.

Pruning also helps improve air circulation within the plant, reducing the risk of fungal infections. Always use sharp, clean tools for cuts, which minimize stress on the plant.

Pro Tip: Roger Cook, This Old House landscape contractor, advises against over-shearing arborvitaes with electric trimmers, which can create a dense outer shell that blocks sunlight from reaching the interior. As he explains, heavy pruning on top makes the plant “thick and it’s not allowing any sunlight into the middle.” If you look inside an over-sheared arborvitae, you’ll find “no leaves, just wood,” which “makes it very hard to prune back.” Instead, he recommends a rejuvenation approach: “Take out one third of the oldest stems making holes or windows to allow the sunlight to get into the center of the hedge.” Watering and Fertilizing

Good watering and fertilization practices can also help your arborvitaes thrive:

Avoid over-fertilizing, as this can lead to weak, spindly growth.

Once established, arborvitaes are relatively drought-tolerant but benefit from occasional deep watering during dry spells.

Apply a slow-release, balanced fertilizer in early spring before new growth begins.

Water deeply and regularly during the first growing season to establish a strong root system.

In sandy or poor soil conditions, consider amending the soil with organic matter or compost to enhance nutrient availability and moisture retention. Protecting Arborvitaes in Winter

Common Arborvitaes Problems and Solutions

While arborvitaes are hardy plants, they can face challenges. Here are some common issues and how to address them:

Bagworms: Remove bags by hand or use appropriate insecticides if infestations are severe.

Deer damage: Choose deer-resistant varieties like the Thuja Green Giant or use repellents and natural barriers, such as ivory soap.

Root rot: Make sure the tree has proper drainage and avoid overwatering to prevent this fungal disease.

Yellowing foliage: This could indicate overwatering, nutrient deficiency, or pest infestation. Adjust watering practices, apply a balanced fertilizer, or inspect for pests. Seasonal Care Tips

Arborvitaes appreciate seasonal care to remain healthy year-round. In spring, apply a balanced fertilizer and prune as needed. During summer, regular watering is essential, especially in hot, dry conditions. In fall, a fresh layer of mulch helps insulate the roots, and inspecting for bagworms prepares the plant for winter.

Arborvitae Varieties for Different Climates

Selecting the right arborvitae variety for your climate is key to successful growth. In colder regions, consider Techny, which have excellent winter hardiness. For climates with heat and humidity, Green Giant and Steeplechase perform well. Companion Planting with Arborvitaes
