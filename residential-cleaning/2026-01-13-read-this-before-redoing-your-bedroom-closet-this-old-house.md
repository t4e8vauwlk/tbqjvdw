A well-organized bedroom closet makes it easier to find what you need, removing stress from your daily routine. Whether you’re planning a major overhaul or looking for some simple redesign tips, we’ll help you through each step of a closet remodel so you can create a stylish storage space that meets your needs. Your Current Closet Situation

Before starting on your closet remodel, evaluate your existing space and storage needs. This will help you make better decisions about your new closet’s design and organization.

Eric Larsen | Illustrated by Eric Larsen

Take Stock of What You Have in Your Closet

Start by decluttering and organizing your belongings. Sell, donate, or get rid of items you no longer need or use. Then, follow these steps for your remaining stuff:

Separate hanging clothes into two piles: short items (shirts, folded pants) and long items (dresses, coats, pants hung full-length).

Measure the height of each pile to determine the required lengths for short and long hanging rods.

Arrange foldable items in 10-inch-high stacks, allowing 14 inches of shelf length for each stack.

Take note of accessories like ties, belts, and shoes that will need dedicated storage space.

Pro Tip: Interior designer Melissa Gulley, who collaborated with the This Old House team on closet organization, uses a design tool that lets homeowners visualize their closet layout—changing colors, swapping in shoe shelves or drawers—and prints out specifications with a shopping list. Measure Your Existing Space

Taking accurate measurements wil help you plan your new closet layout. Here’s what to do:

Measure the width, depth, and height of the entire closet space.

Note the location and size of any obstacles, such as electrical outlets, light switches, or HVAC vents.

Measure the width of your closet door opening to check that new organizers or built-ins will fit through.

If you have sliding doors, measure the overlap to account for reduced accessibility. Ideal Closet Dimensions and Design

The ideal reach-in closet is typically 6–8 feet wide and 24–30 inches deep. This size allows for ample storage while still being accessible. Here are some key measurements to keep in mind:

Standard double doors are best for easy access, assuming there’s room to swing them open.

The inside of return walls (where doors are hinged) should be no longer than 18 inches to prevent blind alleys.

Allow at least 24 inches of rod length per person for adequate hanging space.

Shelf depths can range from 12 inches for folded clothes to 16 inches for bulkier items.

Pro Tip: This Old House master carpenter Norm Abram points out that many older bedroom closets are only 12 to 14 inches deep—far too shallow for proper storage. “For a proper closet, you need about 24,” Norm advises. Closet Configurations

A good closet layout needs strategically placing rods, shelves, and storage units to maximize space. Consider these configurations:

Install double hanging rods to double your hanging space for shorter items.

Place a high shelf (at least 80 inches from the floor) to store seasonal or less-used items.

Allocate space for a shoe rack or shelving unit near the bottom of the closet.

If space allows, include a small dresser or drawers for folded items.

Use the vertical space efficiently with adjustable shelving systems.

Lighting and Electrical Considerations for Your Closet

Good lighting and electrical planning help create a functional and inviting closet space. Good lighting helps you see your belongings clearly, while strategic electrical access can add convenience to your daily routine. Choose Lighting Fixtures

Effective closet lighting illuminates the entire space without creating harsh shadows:

Install LED strip lighting under shelves for even, shadow-free illumination.

Use recessed lighting in the ceiling for overall brightness.

Consider a decorative fixture in walk-in closets to add style and ambient light.

Avoid bare bulbs, which can be a fire hazard and provide poor lighting. Instead, go for fixtures designed explicitly for closet use that meet local building codes. Rockler Add Outlets for Convenience

Well-placed outlets are convenient for common tasks, like the following:

Install an outlet for charging devices, or even a charging station.

Add an outlet for a steamer if you frequently touch up clothes in the closet.

Consider a built-in ironing board with a nearby outlet.

Always hire a licensed electrician to install new fixtures and outlets to guarantee safety and code compliance. Flooring Options for Your Closet

While often overlooked, flooring plays an important role in your closet’s utility and style. Consider these options:

Hardwood or luxury vinyl plank (LVP) flooring is durable and has a classic look.

Low-pile carpet is comfortable underfoot and can help protect dropped items.

Tile or sealed concrete are excellent choices for easy cleaning and moisture resistance.

Here are some additional design tips for a stylish space that blends with your home:

Choose a flooring color that coordinates with your closet system for a cohesive look.
