If you have curly, textured hair, you know how easily it can turn into a poofy, frizzy mess. In humid weather, in rain, or when you brush it too hard, it just seems to have a mind of its own. But if you're tired of dealing with a big ball of hair, there are ways to de-poof it. While it starts with being smarter about the way you shampoo your hair and ensuring that it's getting enough moisture, you also need to know the right styling tricks to keep your hair looking smooth and fabulous rather than frizzy and out of control. Steps Part 1 Part 1 of 3: Shampooing Your Hair to De-poof It Download Article 1

Avoid shampooing your hair every day. Hair gets poofy and frizzy when it’s dry. While shampooing your hair cleans it, it also strips your locks of the natural oils that keep it moisturized and more manageable. To keep your hair from getting poofy, shampoo it every other day at most.

If you already shampoo your hair every other day and it’s still poofy, try shampooing every two days instead.

When your hair looks a little greasy between shampoos, add a little dry shampoo to freshen it up. 2

Use a moisturizing shampoo. When you do shampoo your hair, it’s important to use the right products to keep it from getting too big and poofy. Your best bet is a moisturizing shampoo that will clean your hair while still keeping it hydrated. Look for a shampoo that’s infused with moisturizing ingredients, such as argan oil, macadamia oil, vitamin-E, and other emollients.

Sulfate-free shampoos benefit curly hair types and longer hair.

You can also use a smoothing shampoo, which is designed to handle humidity.[1]XExpert SourceShun Pittman

Blot your hair to dry it. When you’re finished shampooing your hair, drying it in the right way can help keep it from getting poofy or frizzy. Rubbing a towel over your hair can cause breakage that makes it look poofy. Instead of rubbing, gently blot the towel over your locks to get rid of the excess water.[2]XResearch source

It’s best not to twist the towel around your hair either. That can also lead to frizz.

For the best results, use a microfiber towel to dry your hair. It creates less friction so your hair isn’t as likely to become poofy. 4

Shampoo your hair at night. When you want to keep your hair from getting poofy, you’re better off letting it air dry rather than blow drying it. That’s why it’s best to shampoo your hair before you go to bed so it has all night to dry. Shampoo at least 30 minutes before you plan to go to sleep, though.[3]XResearch source

To wake up with the most manageable hair, apply a styling cream from mid-length to tip after shampooing it. Next, pull your hair back into a tight bun at the nape of your neck and go to sleep. In the morning, undo the bun and you’ll have soft, sleek waves. Run your fingers through them to gently separate them. Advertisement Part 2 Part 2 of 3: Moisturizing Your Hair to De-poof It Download Article 1

Condition your hair every time you shampoo. Your hair is more likely to become poofy if it’s dehydrated.[4]XExpert SourceShun Pittman

Master Cosmetologist, Global Salon Educator & AuthorExpert Interview To make sure that it has enough moisture, be sure to follow up every shampoo with a rich conditioner. Look for a formula that contains proteins, natural oils, and emollients such as shea butter.

For the most hydrated hair, leave your conditioner on for 3 to 5 minutes in the shower, or as long as the manufacturer recommends. That will give the moisturizing ingredients enough time to penetrate your hair. 2

Apply a leave-in conditioner. While a traditional conditioner will help moisturize your hair, you ultimately rinse it out so your hair may start to get dry and poofy over time.[5]XExpert SourceShun Pittman

Master Cosmetologist, Global Salon Educator & AuthorExpert Interview To keep it sleek, use a leave-in conditioner that will hydrate your hair throughout the day. Work it through your hair while it’s still damp to lock in the moisture.[6]XResearch source

If your hair is thick and coarse, use a lotion or cream leave-in conditioner.

If your hair is fine, use a spray leave-in conditioner. 3

Use a conditioning hair mask regularly. Even using a traditional conditioner and leave-in conditioner every time you shampoo your hair may not be enough to prevent it from becoming dry and poofy. Incorporate a deep conditioner or hair mask into your hair care routine for an intense dose of hydration at least once a week.[7]XResearch source

Follow the instructions on the hair mask’s packaging to determine the proper way to use it. In most cases, you apply it to wet hair after you’ve shampooed it and allow it to sit for 20 to 30 minutes before rinsing it out.

Wear a shower cap or plastic wrap over the hair mask to create some heat when you’re wearing it. That will help it penetrate the hair more easily, so it is fully hydrated.

If you hair gets extremely poofy, you may want to use a deep conditioner every time that you shampoo your hair. You can also wear the mask overnight and shampoo it out in the morning for more hydration. Just be sure to cover your head to keep the mask from getting on your sheets. Advertisement Part 3 Part 3 of 3: Styling Your Hair to De-poof It Download Article 1

Get the right haircut. Some haircuts can encourage frizziness and leave you with poofy hair. To keep your locks from getting poofy, avoid short and heavily layered styles. Instead, opt for styles that feature blunt ends with just a few long layers. The length and weight of the cut will help keep your hair from poofing up.

You should also avoid texturizing, thinning or razor cutting, which has a tendency to promote frizziness. 2

Detangle your hair with a wide-tooth comb. When you get out of the shower, don’t use a brush to detangle your hair. Brushes are too rough and can snag your hair when it’s at its most fragile. Instead, use a wide tooth comb to gently work through the tangles so your hair doesn’t become frizzy.

A wooden comb is the best option because it won’t generate static electricity, which can lead to frizz.

When you need to use a brush on dry hair, opt for a natural bristle style. They are less rough on your hair than synthetic bristles. 3

Apply an oil or anti-frizz product. If your hair is dry, it will soak up all of the moisture in the air, which means that it can become super poofy in humid weather. To ensure that your hair behaves no matter what the weather, use a hair oil, such as argan, jojoba, or coconut, or anti-frizz serum to seal and protect your hair.[8]XResearch source

If you’re applying oil to wet hair, place a few drops in your palm, rub it between your hands, and gently smooth it throughout your hair.

Try tying your hair up into a sleek ponytail. Then, smooth it down with styling gel so your hair withstands humidity throughout the day.[9]XExpert SourceShun Pittman

If you’re using oil on dry hair, apply a drop to the tips of your fingers. Gently work it through the ends of your hair from the ears down to fight the poofiness. 4

Use a heat protectant before heat styling. Heat styling isn’t a good idea if your hair is prone to poofiness -- but using a flat iron can give you sleek, smooth hair, so straightening it can be tempting. If you must heat style your hair, always use a heat protectant product beforehand. It coats your hair so none of its natural moisture is striped when you use the heat.

For thick, coarse hair, look for a lotion or cream heat protectant.
