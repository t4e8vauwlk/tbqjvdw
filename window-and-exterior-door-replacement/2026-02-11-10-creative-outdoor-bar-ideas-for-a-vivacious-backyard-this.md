# 10 Creative Outdoor Bar Ideas for a Vivacious Backyard - This Old House

Skip to content Julien Fernandez/GAP Interiors

An outdoor bar can transform your backyard into a great entertaining space for friends and family. With an outdoor bar, you can create a focal point for gatherings while designing your outdoor living space however you want. From permanent structures to portable ones, we’ve compiled outdoor bar ideas for different styles, spaces, and budgets.

Understanding the Appeal of Outdoor Bars

Outdoor bars have become more popular with homeowners wanting to maximize their outdoor living space. They provide a dedicated area of the home for entertaining, blending some indoor comfort with the outdoors. Outdoor bars reduce foot traffic into the house while still allowing you to have a space for socializing and hosting guests on a nice evening.

Expert Insight: Jay Haverson, a Greenwich, Connecticut, architect who has designed numerous built-in bars, explains the trend:“For many homeowners, it’s an answer to the old, ‘Every time I have a party, everyone ends up in the kitchen.'” Moving the bar outside takes that same principle a step further—drawing guests out of the house entirely and into a dedicated entertaining zone. Before exploring specific outdoor bar ideas, consider the components that make these spaces functional and inviting. When planning your outdoor bar, consider seating, storage, and lighting. Bar Counter and Seating

The bar counter will likely be the centerpiece of your outdoor bar, serving as a preparation area and seating space. Since you’re building an outdoor bar, you’ll want to choose a durable and weather-resistant counter material, such as stone, concrete, or treated wood. For seating, you should also consider weather-resistant options while factoring in comfort and design.

Pro Tip: This Old House master carpenter Norm Abram is fond of teak for outdoor furniture—even though its preternatural hardness “completely knocks the edge off my planer knives.” Redwood, cedar, and cypress are also on his short list because they resist rot and insects, making them excellent choices for bar stools and counter seating. Storage and Refrigeration

Storage spaces are great for outdoor bars. Having a place that’s easily accessible with your essentials can prevent trips to and from your home. You may want to invest in a small refrigerator or cooler to keep beverages and snacks chilled, especially on warmer evenings. Lighting and Ambiance

Proper lighting creates the right atmosphere for your outdoor bar while brightening up your space when it gets dark outside. Overhead lighting is most commonly used, but you can also add task lighting near the bar counter for better visibility. For portable options, string lights, lanterns, or LED strip lights are a good option.

Pro Tip: Lighting pro Paul Gosselin recommends commercial-grade M5 LED string lights in warm white for wrapping pergolas or stringing over a bar area: “They won’t get hot and harm greenery like incandescents and can be left out year-round.” To save yourself hours of labor, tack up strings along beams in a zigzag pattern using a staple gun fitted with a wire attachment, which has a groove that centers the wire. For homeowners looking to design a permanent bar structure, there are many construction options while considering durability and style. These options can be customized to fit your specific needs and design preferences. Yardistry Structures Gazebo Bar

Gazebo bars combine the charm of a classic garden structure with the functionality of outdoor bars. Gazebos are freestanding, roof-covered spaces, usually with columns and high ceilings. These structures have ample space, allowing you to personalize the area as you wish with customized decoration, flooring, and paint colors.

Expert Insight: Joan Honeyman, a landscape architect in Washington, D.C., told This Old House Magazine: “A lot of times a gazebo is too small, but it’s not often that it’s too big.” She advises that while a gazebo design needs to blend with the landscape and work with the style of the house, size is a major consideration—especially when you’re planning to outfit the space as a bar with seating and serving areas. Pergola Bar

Pergolas have a more open and airy feel compared to gazebos, usually blending with the outdoors. A pergola bar typically has four columns supporting an open-raftered roof. Many pergola designs now include built-in bar counters, making them a convenient option for outdoor entertaining. Kitchen Window Bar

For a space- and money-saving solution that connects your indoor and outdoor areas, you can install a bar counter outside your kitchen window. This design lets you prepare snacks and drinks from your kitchen and hand them to guests outside. Choose a window that opens easily and is accessible from inside and outside before installing a weather-resistant countertop. Portable and Space-Saving Bar Options

If you’re working with limited space or prefer flexibility in your outdoor setup, portable and space-saving bar options offer creative solutions for entertaining. Pushcarts, Murphy bars, and bar sheds are versatile options for your own convenience when designing your outdoor space. Laura Moss Pushcart Bar

Pushcart bars provide great flexibility for outdoor entertaining. They can be easily moved around your yard or patio for different occasions. Options range from simple metal carts to custom designs with built-in storage and preparation surfaces. For a DIY project, consider repurposing furniture or building your own wooden pushcart to create a custom bar on wheels.

Pro Tip: This Old House contributor Deborah Snoonian designed a mobile outdoor bar cart built from cedar that’s tough enough to withstand sun, rain, and spills. The two-wheeled construction lets you navigate stone patios or lumpy lawns with ease, the top tray is removable for serving drinks or munchies, and the entire thing can be easily taken apart and stored flat. Expect about 10 hours of build time at a moderate difficulty level—making accurate measurements and notching the legs requires precision and patience. Murphy Bar

If you’re concerned about space when contemplating building an outdoor entertaining area, Murphy bars may be your best option. Once installed, these bars can be folded up against an exterior wall when not in use. This saves space on your patio or in your yard while still having a bar area when hosting guests. Bar Shed

Practical Considerations for Outdoor Bars

When building and designing an outdoor bar, you want a space that remains functional and attractive despite outdoor weather conditions. Weather-proofing and regular maintenance will help keep your outdoor space in top condition. Weather-Proofing Your Bar

Outdoor bars are an investment, so choosing weather-resistant materials and incorporating features, such as a roof or awning, can shield your bar from outdoor elements while still being practical and comfortable. You can also purchase and use covers for your bar equipment and furniture when you’re not using the space. If you plan to install or use appliances in the bar area, be sure to select outdoor-rated appliances and extension cords if needed. Maintenance and Upkeep

Regular maintenance will keep your outdoor bar safe and functional. Developing a routine for cleaning surfaces, checking for wear and tear, and addressing any issues will help your space stay clean. Store cushions and other fabric items indoors during harsh weather, and apply protective sealants to wood and stone surfaces as needed. Improving Your Outdoor Bar

DIY outdoor bar projects can be simple and rewarding for homeowners looking to save money or add a personal touch. Building bar counters and refurbishing or upcycling furniture are great options to create an outdoor bar while saving money on new furniture and construction. Building a Simple Bar Counter

You can create a basic outdoor bar by building a sturdy counter using weather-resistant materials like treated lumber or concrete blocks. Add a smooth, waterproof top surface and storage shelves or cabinets underneath. This project can be easily customized to fit your space and skill level. Upcycling Furniture into Bar Elements

Old furniture that has seen better days can be repurposed into bar components. For example, with just a few adjustments and changes, you can transform an old dresser into a bar cabinet or use a vintage door as a bar top. By sanding, painting, and refinishing old furniture, you can personally design your outdoor bar space. Upcycled furniture adds character and reduces waste while also being more cost-effective.
