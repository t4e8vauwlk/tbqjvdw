What Is a Steam Shower and How Does It Work?

A steam shower is a type of bathing enclosure that generates water vapor, creating a warm, humid environment for relaxation and therapeutic purposes. Unlike traditional showers, steam showers use a specialized system to produce and distribute steam throughout the enclosed space. Components of a Steam Shower System

Courtesy of MrSteam | Photo courtesy of MrSteam

A typical steam shower system consists of four main components:

Generator: This is the heart of the system, responsible for boiling water to create steam. About the size of a briefcase, it can be installed up to 60 feet away from the shower in a dry, heated location with proper ventilation.

Controls: Installed inside the shower, away from direct steam flow, the control panel allows you to manage various aspects of your steam session, including temperature, duration, and additional features like music or aromatherapy.

Steamhead: This is where the steam is released into the shower. It’s typically installed on the shower wall, 6–12 inches above the floor and opposite the seating area.

Plumbing and power: The system runs on low-voltage household electric power (208v or 240v) and requires a water line to the generator, a steam line from the generator to the shower, and a drain line. The Science Behind Steam Therapy
