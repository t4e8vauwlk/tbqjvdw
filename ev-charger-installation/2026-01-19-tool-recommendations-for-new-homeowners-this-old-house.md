# Tool Recommendations for New Homeowners - This Old House

Skip to content

Owning a home comes with a unique set of responsibilities, including maintenance and repairs. Having the right tools on hand can make these tasks much easier and more manageable.

In the video above, This Old House experts Tom Silva, Richard Trethewey, and Jenn Nawada share their top tool recommendations for new homeowners. Whether you’re a first time do-it-yourselfer (DIYer) or looking to upgrade your toolkit, this guide will help you identify the essentials.

Assessing Your Skill Level and Needs as a Homeowner

Before purchasing tools, it’s important to consider your skill level and the projects types you’re likely to tackle. This will help you make informed decisions about which tools to invest in and how much to spend. Matching Tools to Your Abilities

When selecting tools, consider your experience and comfort level with various home improvement tasks. Silva says you should understand the skill level of the person who will be using them. Beginners may want to start with basic, user-friendly options, while those with more experience might benefit from more specialized tools. Identifying Common Household Projects

If you’re buying for yourself, think about what you’re likely to encounter as a homeowner. Common tasks include the following: Assembling furniture Basic plumbing repairs Hanging pictures and shelves Minor electrical work Yard maintenance

By anticipating your needs, you can prioritize which tools to acquire first.

Essential Power Tools for Every Homeowner

Power tools can significantly speed up many home improvement tasks. Here are our experts’ recommendations for must-have power tools for new homeowners. Drill/Driver

A drill/driver is perhaps the most versatile and essential power tool for any homeowner. A good drill driver can both drill holes and drive screws, making it indispensable for a wide range of projects. Look for a cordless model with a rechargeable battery for maximum convenience.

Pro Tip: Tom Silva, This Old House contractor, is a firm believer in going cordless: “I don’t even use a corded model anymore. Unless I’ve got a pile of drywall to hang or a whole subfloor to screw off, it’s faster to grab a cordless and go.” He notes that the shift isn’t limited to carpenters: “Plumbers, electricians, the guys who do the ductwork—they’ve all gone cordless.” Other Useful Power Tools

When choosing power tools, opt for those with rechargeable, interchangeable batteries to save money and storage space. Most major power tool manufacturers like DeWalt, Ryobi, and Milwaukee offer some kind of combo set that includes a drill/driver and is likely to include some of the following as well. Circular saw Cut-off grinder Impact driver Jigsaw Orbital sander Oscillating multitool Reciprocating saw

A good tape measure is crucial for any measuring task. Silva recommends getting a tape measure that’s at least 16 feet long to handle most household measuring needs. Level

A level helps you find true horizontal and true vertical, ensuring that your shelves, pictures, and other installations are straight. Invest in a quality torpedo level, as Silva advises against cheap options that may not be accurate. Utility Knife

A sharp utility knife is useful for a variety of tasks, from opening packages to cutting drywall. Look for a model with replaceable blades for long-term use.

Pro Tip: Norm Abram recommends choosing a utility knife with built-in safety features. “It’s safer than using a kitchen knife,” he says of keeping a retractable utility knife on hand for opening boxes and other lightweight tasks. He suggests looking for a model that has a lock, a safety guard, and a blade you can change without a screwdriver. Pliers

Even if you’re not a plumbing expert, having a few basic plumbing tools can help you handle minor issues and emergencies. Here are Trethewey’s recommendations. Wrenches

As Trethewey says, wrenches are essential for many plumbing tasks like tightening or loosening nuts and bolts. He recommends having either a couple of adjustable wrenches or a full set of box (fixed) wrenches. This will ensure you have the right size for the task whenever you need it. Plumber’s Pliers

Also known as channel-lock or tongue-and-groove pliers, these are specifically designed for plumbing tasks. These pliers’ jaws are set at an angle to the handle, allowing you to work on pipe connections in tight spaces. A plier set may include plumber’s pliers or you may need to purchase them separately. Plunger

While not mentioned in the video, a good plunger is an essential tool for every homeowner to clear minor clogs in toilets and drains. Make sure to pick a plunger that has a flange, or soft rubber flap, extending from the cup for use on toilets. A standard plunger is only useful against flat surfaces like the bottom of a kitchen sink.

Pro Tip: Richard Trethewey, This Old House plumbing and heating expert, emphasizes that the very first thing a new homeowner should do is locate the main water shutoff valve: “When you first moved into a house, somebody should go down to the basement or in wherever the main water shower valve is and put a tag that lets you know. So when you see water come out of a wall that shouldn’t have water in it, you know to go down and take this handle and turn it.” Knowing where to shut off the water in an emergency can save you from catastrophic damage.

Backyard Landscaping and Gardening Tools

Maintaining your outdoor space is an important part of homeownership. Here are some essential tools for landscaping and gardening. Pruners

As landscape expert Jenn Nawada demonstrates, a high-quality pair of pruners is essential for maintaining your yard. Look for heavy-duty pruners that can handle both perennials and woody shrubs. Other Important Landscaping Tools

While not specifically mentioned, these tools are also crucial for yard maintenance: Garden hose Lawn mower Rake Shovel

Investing in quality landscaping tools will make outdoor maintenance tasks easier and more efficient.

Pro Tip: This Old House landscaper Lee Gilliam advises a steady approach to yard care: “Except maybe in the dead of winter, there’s always something that needs attention outdoors. Doing a little work a few times a month is always better than trying to get the job done all at once—and all you need is some basic tools.” Multitools and Versatile Options

For homeowners who want to maximize utility while minimizing storage space, multitools can be an excellent option. Multitool

Trethewey highly recommends a multitool, which he carries with him at all times. These compact tools often include pliers, knives, screwdrivers, and other useful implements in one portable package. Tool Sets

Consider purchasing comprehensive tool sets that include a variety of commonly used tools. These sets often come in a carrying case, making storage and organization easier. However, if budget is an issue, it’s typically better to purchase a few quality tools rather than a full set of cheaply made ones.

Investing in Quality Tools for Your Home

Throughout their discussion, the experts stress the importance of investing in high-quality tools. While it may be tempting to choose cheaper options, quality tools offer the following advantages: Better performance Improved safety Longer lifespan More comfortable user experience

By investing in quality tools, you’ll save money in the long run and be better equipped to handle a wide range of home maintenance tasks. When shopping for tools, consider the following tips to ensure you’re getting high-quality options.

Read reviews: Look for tools with positive reviews from other homeowners and professionals.

Pay attention to materials: Tools made of metal are far more likely to hold up to years of use than those with plastic casings or handles. However, plastic is substantially lighter than metal, and the best tool is one you’ll use.

Check warranties: Quality tools often come with warranties that protect against defects and guarantee performance.

Consider brands: Some brands are known for their commitment to quality and durability. While brand name isn’t everything, it can be a helpful indicator.
